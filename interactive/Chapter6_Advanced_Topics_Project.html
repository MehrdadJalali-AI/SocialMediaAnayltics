Computed in Python: TfidfVectorizer(min_df=5, max_df=0.5) with a custom stopword list that keeps negation, NMF(n_components=k, random_state=42), on the 2,363 deduplicated posts. Choose k, read the representative posts, and give each topic a name.

Four checks per topic

Top words — do they belong together? · Representative posts — read 5–10 · Size — tiny topics may be noise · Distinct — different from the others?

Bad vs. better names

“Topic 1” → “Waiting at chargers” · “Battery” → “Battery recall and safety” · “Money” → “Subsidy and price debate” · “Positive” → “Driving experience and range”

The model finds patterns — people give them names.Too few topics merge themes; too many fragment them. Try several k and choose the one you can name.

Section 4

Beyond text: images, conversations, events — and bots

“Very disappointed.” + a photo of a broken charger. Image and text together carry the meaning — text-only models miss half the post. Multimodal models exist — validate carefully.

“Exactly what I expected.” — sincere or sarcastic? The parent post often decides. Add the parent text as model input; sample whole threads, not single posts.

A recall on 4 March changes everything after it. Compare before/after windows, mark known events on every time plot — and beware: events change who posts.

PhenomenonPossible signalsCaution
Misinformationclaims contradicted by reliable sources; fast, deep cascades (Ch 5)labels need fact-checks, not a model’s opinion
Botsposting around the clock, extreme volume, identical texts, new accountsmany real users look “bot-like”; detection tools disagree
Coordinated campaignsmany accounts posting the same text or link within minutescoordination ≠ inauthenticity (fan clubs, activists)
Signal (evidence) or verdict (claim)?
Describe the pattern — be careful with the label.“37 identical posts from 12 accounts within 10 minutes” is evidence. “These are foreign bots” is a claim you cannot support.

A strong research question

WeakWhy it is weakStronger

from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import TfidfVectorizer
from sklearn.decomposition import NMF
tfidf = TfidfVectorizer(min_df=5, max_df=0.5, stop_words=my_stopwords)
X = tfidf.fit_transform(df["text_classical"])
nmf = NMF(n_components=6, random_state=42)
W = nmf.fit_transform(X)                    # posts x topics
df["topic"] = W.argmax(axis=1)
words = tfidf.get_feature_names_out()
for k, comp in enumerate(nmf.components_):
    print(k, [words[i] for i in comp.argsort()[-8:]])
pd.crosstab(df["topic"], df["sentiment"], normalize="index")
