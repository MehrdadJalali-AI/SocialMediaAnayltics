Transformers & LLMs for Sentiment, Emotion and Stance

In Chapter 2 we built two transparent baselines — VADER and TF-IDF + Logistic Regression. They were fast and explainable, but they missed context: sarcasm, negation scope, informal expressions. Today we use models that read words in context, and large language models as classifiers. A transformer must beat the baseline on the same test posts — and we must report what it costs.

def preprocess(text):            # from the Cardiff NLP model card
    out = []
    for t in text.split(" "):
        t = "@user" if t.startswith("@") and len(t) > 1 else t
        t = "http" if t.startswith("http") else t
        out.append(t)
    return " ".join(out)

Keep

emojis, punctuation, capitals, word order

Replace

usernames and links, as the model card says

Avoid

stemming, stop-word removal, lowercasing

Do not over-clean — context is the model’s input.That is why Chapter 2 created a separate text_transformer column.

Section 10 · Part B

LLMs as classifiers and annotators

Where LLMs help

No labels yet
classify stance or emotion with a clear prompt
Labelling support
draft labels that humans then check
Explanations
a short reason speeds up error analysis
Rare categories
describe a new label in words instead of collecting data

What to watch

Inconsistency
the same post can get different labels on reruns
Model drift
API models change; results may not be reproducible
Privacy
sending posts to an external API is a GDPR question
Cost & speed
thousands of posts × tokens × price
Confident errors
fluent explanations can still be wrong
An LLM label is a prediction — validate it like any other model.

Section 13

Multilingual posts: three strategies

“Das Update ist echt mies, but the camera is 🔥” — 24% of the EV posts are German and 8% mix languages.

How: machine translation, then an English model. Strength: the best English models. Risk: tone, slang and sarcasm get lost in translation.

How: one model for all languages (e.g. XLM-T). Strength: keeps the original text; handles code-switching. Risk: weaker for small languages — check per-language F1.

How: detect the language, route to a German or English model. Strength: strong when each language has enough data. Risk: short posts are hard to language-detect.

Section 19

Theory summary

1 · Context matters

contextual embeddings fix many baseline errors — not label problems.

2 · Three ways to use a model

inference, fine-tuning or zero-shot — chosen by data and cost.

3 · Light preprocessing

keep emojis and word order; follow the model card.

4 · LLMs need validation

gold sample, agreement, consistency, error review.

5 · Languages are a decision

translate, multilingual or per-language — report per language.

6 · Fair comparison

same split, gains next to costs, bias audit and disclosure.
